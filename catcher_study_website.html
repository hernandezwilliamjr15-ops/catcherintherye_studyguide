What's the book about?

The Catcher in the Rye is about Holden Caulfield, a teenager who leaves his school and spends a few days by himself in New York City. While he is there, he thinks about his family, people he knows, growing up, and things in life that bother or confuse him.

Holden tells the story himself, so we get to hear exactly what he thinks. At the same time, readers have to pay attention to what his words show about his personality and feelings.

Quick facts

Author: J. D. Salinger
Published: 1951
Main character: Holden Caulfield
Main setting: New York City
Point of view: First person

Reading tip: This guide uses chapter numbers because page numbers are different in each edition of the book.

10 things to know

Study guide / chapter examples
01

Holden Caulfield

Holden notices a lot, makes sarcastic comments, and judges the people around him pretty often. He can be funny, but you can also tell that he has a hard time understanding himself and the people in his life.

Text evidence: Chapters 1–3 show how Holden talks and how quickly he starts criticizing Pencey Prep and the people at the school.
Character
02

Phoniness

Holden calls people fake when he thinks they are just trying to impress others. However, he also hides his feelings and sometimes acts differently around people, so he is not always that different from the people he criticizes.

Text evidence: Chapters 4–6 show how Holden reacts to Stradlater's date and how he talks about other students at Pencey.
Theme
03

Alienation and loneliness

Even though Holden is around people for most of the story, he still feels alone. He wants someone to understand him, but he also pushes people away and has trouble trusting them.

Text evidence: In Chapters 9–14, Holden goes around New York and tries talking to different people, but most of these moments make him feel even more alone.
Theme
04

Innocence and childhood

Holden likes being around children because he thinks they are more honest and less complicated than adults. His memories of Allie and the way he cares about Phoebe show that he wants to protect kids and their innocence.

Text evidence: Chapters 16–17 show Holden thinking about the children at the museum, while Chapters 21–22 show how close he is to Phoebe.
Theme
05

The catcher image

Holden pictures himself standing in a field of rye, catching children before they fall off a cliff. This shows that he wants to keep kids safe and protect them from having to grow up too fast.

Text evidence: In Chapter 22, Holden explains this idea to Phoebe. It shows what he wishes he could do with his life.
Symbolism
06

Allie and grief

Holden's younger brother Allie died before the story begins. Holden still thinks about him a lot, and it is clear that losing him is one reason Holden feels so sad and angry.

Text evidence: Chapter 5 is about Holden remembering Allie and the baseball mitt that Allie wrote poems on.
Character / memory
07

Relationships and trust

Holden wants to have real relationships with people, but he does not always know how to open up or accept help. His interactions with Sally, his memories of Jane, and his time with Phoebe show this in different ways.

Text evidence: Chapters 17–18 cover Holden's date with Sally, and Chapters 21–23 show how much Phoebe means to him.
Relationships
08

Growing up and adulthood

Holden thinks being an adult means dealing with fake people and disappointment, so he does not want to grow up. But as the story goes on, he starts to see that change is something he cannot avoid.

Text evidence: Chapters 20–22 show Holden going through a really difficult time before he spends time with Phoebe, who makes him think differently.
Coming of age
09

The Museum of Natural History

Holden likes the museum because everything inside stays the same whenever he visits. It makes him feel comfortable because he wishes the rest of the world would stop changing too.

Text evidence: In Chapter 16, Holden remembers going to the museum and explains why he likes seeing the same displays.
Symbolism / setting
10

The ending

At the end of the book, Holden watches Phoebe ride the carousel and is happy just watching her. This moment could show that he is starting to understand that kids need to take chances and grow up in their own way.

Text evidence: Chapter 25 ends with the carousel scene, and Chapter 26 shows Holden looking back on everything that happened.
Ending / change

Why people still relate to it today

Then & now

Feeling like nobody understands you

A lot of teenagers can understand how Holden feels when it seems like nobody gets him. Even if someone is around friends, classmates, or people online all day, they can still feel left out or alone.

Figuring out who is being real

Holden gets annoyed when people act fake or only care about how others see them. This can connect to social media today, where people usually post only the best parts of their lives.

Worrying about the future

Holden does not like thinking about becoming an adult. Students today can feel that same pressure when they think about school, jobs, relationships, and what they want to do after graduation.

Dealing with loss and change

The way Holden remembers Allie shows how losing someone can change the way a person sees things. It also reminds us that people might be dealing with a lot even when they do not show it.

Discussion question: Do you think Holden is just angry at the world, or is he upset because he wishes people and life were better?
